This project analyses customer satisfaction and product recommendation behaviour in the beauty industry using the Kaggle "Sephora Products and Skincare Reviews" dataset. I began with an initial inspection of the first 1,000 reviews and found that the sample contained only two products and two brands (999 LANEIGE reviews and one NUDESTIX review), so it could not represent the dataset. I therefore combined all five review files (1,094,411 reviews covering 2,351 products and 142 brands) and repeated the analysis in Python using Pandas, NumPy, SciPy and Matplotlib in Google Colab. The original 1,000-row results were recomputed and matched exactly, and were then compared with the full-dataset results.

The analysis shows that ratings are strongly skewed towards the top of the scale (63.9% of reviews give 5 stars and 82.1% give 4 or 5 stars), and that recommendation rate rises sharply with star rating, from 0.8% at 1 star to 99.9% at 5 stars. This is an association, not proof of causation. I also compared ratings across brands, products, price bands and customer characteristics, and used keyword groups to identify recurring themes in 114,061 low-rated reviews, such as "no visible effect", irritation, breakouts and price. Limitations include self-selected reviewers, missing recommendation values (15.4%), very unequal review counts per product, and approximate keyword matching. No machine-learning model was built.

The report defines the business problem, objectives, research questions and hypotheses, documents data cleaning, presents seven figures and thirteen tables, a step-by-step analytical approach with a justification for each method, industry and academic research context, and suggests business recommendations and next steps such as topic modelling and statistical tests of product differences.
